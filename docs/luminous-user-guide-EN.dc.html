Basics
Getting started
Luminous plays the music already on your computer. Just point it at a folder once, and it keeps watching for new files from then on.
1
Open Settings → Folders and add the folder your music lives in.
2
Luminous scans it and then keeps watching — drop a new album in and it shows up on its own.
3
Everything lands under Collection in the sidebar, sorted into Artists, Albums, and Songs.
4
Moved or renamed a file on disk? Luminous notices the change on its own and updates your library — no rescan needed.
Home is the first thing you see: your top artists, the songs you've played most, and what's been added recently.
Basics
Your collection
Collection in the sidebar expands into three views — Artists, Albums, and Songs — each showing a running count and a Sort control in the top-right:
Albums: album name, artist name, year, number of songs.
Artists: artist name, genre, number of songs.
Songs: title, artist, album, track number, duration.
The Songs view lists your whole library as a sortable table. Click Columns to add or remove which fields are shown.
Open an album to see its full tracklist, plus a side panel with the file details for whatever song is currently playing — format, bitrate, sample rate, and loudness. Play all starts at track one; Shuffle and play randomizes the order; the + button adds that album or song to the active playlist — the Queue, by default.
Click the pencil icon next to Play all to open Edit Album Tags — fix the title, artist, genre, or release year for every track on the album at once. The same editor is available per-track from the row's own pencil icon. Lookup AcoustID tries to match the song and retrieve the correct metadata tags automatically.
An artist page groups their work into Albums, EPs, and Singles under a Discography tab strip. Below the Discography is any playlists that include the artist.

Search & browsing
The search bar at the top of the window looks across songs, albums, and artists at once — you never have to think about where something is filed before you go looking for it.
•
The three icons beside the search bar toggle the left and right side panels, or toggle immersive Now Playing mode. Their shortcuts are Ctrl1, Ctrl2, and Ctrl3.
•
The Luminous logo reacts to music playing — click it to toggle this on or off.
1
Press CtrlL (or click the search bar) from anywhere in Luminous.
2
Start typing — matching songs, albums, artists, and playlists appear as you go.
3
The back and forward arrows beside the search bar step through wherever you've navigated, just like a browser.
4
Click the folder icon to open a song from anywhere on your computer.
Basics
Now Playing & the mini player
A player bar sits at the bottom of the window when you're playing a song, with the waveform, transport controls, volume, and a shortcut into the full Now Playing view.
•
Click the waveform to jump to a different point in the song.
•
Click the icon next to the waveform to toggle showing the moodbar instead. The moodbar's color bands reflect the song's frequency balance — red for bass, greens for mid, blue for treble — with brighter regions carrying more energy.
•
The icon on the far right pops the player out into a small floating mini player — handy for keeping an eye on playback while you work in other apps. CtrlM toggles it from anywhere.
•
Click Shuffle to cycle through five modes: shuffle all, shuffle inside the album, shuffle albums, shuffle artists, and no shuffle.
•
Click Repeat to cycle through five modes: repeat song, repeat album, repeat playlist, repeat one by one, and no repeat.
•
Click the heart to favourite a song, or give it a 1–5 star rating, depending on your settings.
•
Press Ctrl2 anywhere to toggle immersive Now Playing — a distraction-free, full-window version of the view.

Basics
Playlists
There are three kinds: the ones you build by hand, smart ones Luminous keeps filled from a rule you set, and Auto Playlists it builds and refreshes on its own.
•
Custom: create a playlist, name it, then drag songs in from anywhere in your collection.
•
Smart: set a rule like "Genre is Rock and Year is after 2015" and Luminous keeps it up to date as your collection grows.
•
Auto: Genre and Decade playlists build themselves from your library and refresh daily with up to 25 tracks — a Genre or Decade only shows up once it has at least 25 matching songs. Click one to browse its tracks, or hit Refresh anytime for a new selection.
A custom playlist
Editing a smart rule
If you click the + next to a song or album, it goes to your Queue playlist by default. Making a new playlist you want to add to instead? Mark it Active first, and anything you + after that lands there instead.
Auto Playlist tile
Browsing one
Inside an Auto Playlist, flip on Auto-Refill to keep adding songs once you reach the end, on top of its once-a-day refresh — and pick a flavor to bias which songs it picks:
All: any song that matches the playlist.
Favourites: gives preference to songs you've marked as favourites.
Familiar: gives preference to songs you've played often.
Discover: gives preference to songs you haven't played often.
Deep Cuts: gives preference to songs you've almost never played.
Basics
Lyrics
Open Lyrics in the sidebar to follow along with whatever's playing — the current line highlights in sync with the music, with the rest dimmed above and below it. Luminous automatically looks up lyrics for the currently playing song; if it's an instrumental, you can mark it as such so Luminous stops looking.
•
If lyrics haven't been found yet, click Refetch to look them up again.
•
Timing off, or a line wrong? Edit lets you correct the text or nudge the sync yourself.
•
Not all lyrics come with timestamps — when they don't, Luminous shows plain text lyrics instead.
•
Click any line in the lyrics to jump to that point in the song.

Settings
Equalizer
Everything here lives under Settings → Equalizer. You don't need to know anything about audio engineering to use it — the defaults sound good, and every change applies instantly, even mid-song.
•
Flip Enable EQ on, then pick a preset from the dropdown — or switch to 20-band parametric if the default 10 bands aren't precise enough. A Reset Bands button appears in that view to snap everything back to flat.
•
Loudness Normalization evens out the volume jump between quiet and loud tracks, so you're not reaching for the volume knob every other song.
•
Fades & crossfade softens the pause/stop button and can blend the tail of one song into the start of the next.

Settings
UI Themes
Pick between several built-in themes, match your system's light/dark setting, or turn on Dynamic Artwork so the whole app — including Now Playing and the mini player — pulls its color from whatever album art is currently playing. The whole app updates immediately, no restart needed.
Want full control? The Custom Theme Builder lets you set your own colors from scratch.
