Basics
Getting started
Luminous plays the music already on your computer. Just point it at a folder once, and it keeps watching for new files from then on.
1
Open Settings → Folders and add the folder your music lives in.
2
Luminous scans it and then keeps watching — drop a new album in and it shows up on its own.
3
Everything lands under Collection in the sidebar, sorted into Artists, Albums, and Songs.
4
Moved or renamed a file on disk? Luminous notices the change on its own and updates your library — no rescan needed.
Home is the first thing you see: your top artists, the songs you've played most, and what's been added recently.
Basics
Home
Home is the first thing you see when you open Luminous — a running snapshot of your library that updates itself as you listen. The greeting up top changes with the time of day.
•
Top Artists — who you reach for most.
•
Most Played — the songs you've listened to the most.
•
Recently Added — whatever's just landed in your library.
Nothing scanned yet? Home shows a simple prompt instead, with a shortcut to add a folder or open Help.
Basics
Your collection
Collection in the sidebar expands into four views — Artists, Albums, Songs, and Genres — each showing a running count and a Sort control in the top-right:
The Songs view lists your whole library as a sortable table. Click Columns to add or remove which fields are shown — including Play Count and Skip Count, if you want a look at your own listening habits.
The Genres view groups every tag in your library into cards you can arrange yourself. Drag a tag chip onto a card to file it as a sub-genre; right-click a chip for Rename, Promote to top-level genre, or Delete. Songs with no genre tag at all show up in their own "No Genre" group so nothing gets lost. Switch to the flat Tags view (top-right) to see every tag as a simple list instead of the curated hierarchy.
Open an album to see its full tracklist, plus a side panel with the file details for whatever song is currently playing — format, bitrate, sample rate, and loudness. Play all starts at track one; Shuffle and play randomizes the order; the + button adds that album or song to the active playlist — the Queue, by default.
Click the pencil icon next to Play all to open Edit Album Tags — fix the title, artist, genre, or release year for every track on the album at once. The same editor is available per-track from the row's own pencil icon. Lookup AcoustID tries to match the song and retrieve the correct metadata tags automatically.
Artist, Album Artist, Composer, and Genre all accept multiple values — press Enter after each one to add it, and Luminous keeps them as distinct, separately-searchable tags rather than one long string. Each of these fields (plus Title and Album) also has its own Sort As field, so you can file "The Beatles" under "Beatles, The" or a genre like "Gothic Metal" under a broader "Metal" without changing the tag itself. Luminous also reads legacy ID3v1 tags on older MP3s, and Clear Artwork removes embedded cover art from a track in one click.
An artist page groups their work into Albums, EPs, and Singles under a Discography tab strip. Below the Discography is any playlists that include the artist. Click Edit to add a bio, official website, and links to Bandcamp, Spotify, Instagram, and other platforms — they show up as an About panel with clickable links right on the artist page.

Basics
Now Playing & the mini player
A player bar sits at the bottom of the window when you're playing a song, with the waveform, transport controls, volume, and a shortcut into the full Now Playing view.
•
Click the waveform to jump to a different point in the song.
•
Click the icon next to the waveform to toggle showing Frequency Bands instead. It layers all three bands at once — blue for low (bass), amber for mid (vocals/snares/leads), white for high (hi-hats/cymbals/transients) — with taller bands carrying more energy.
•
A small spectrum display next to the volume control gives you a live look at the song's frequencies as it plays.
•
The icon on the far right pops the player out into a small floating mini player — handy for keeping an eye on playback while you work in other apps. CtrlM toggles it from anywhere.
•
Click Shuffle to cycle through five modes:
  • Shuffle All: play all tracks in a randomized order.
  • Shuffle Inside Album: play albums in their normal order, but randomize the tracks within each album.
  • Shuffle Albums: play albums in a randomized order, but keep the tracks within each album in order.
  • Shuffle Artists: play all tracks by an artist before moving to a new, randomly selected artist.
  • Shuffle Off: play tracks in their normal sequential order.
•
Click Repeat to cycle through four modes:
  • Repeat Song: loop the current track indefinitely.
  • Repeat Album: loop the current album indefinitely.
  • Repeat Playlist: loop the current queue or playlist indefinitely.
  • Repeat Off: play through the current queue normally and stop at the end.
•
Click the heart to favourite a song, or give it a 1–5 star rating, depending on your settings.
•
Play/pause, skip, and volume media keys work even when Luminous isn't the focused window, and playback shows up in your system's own media controls — so you can see and steer it without switching back.
Click the album cover art in the player bar to toggle Immersive Mode (Ctrl2 also works); click it again (or from Immersive Mode) to jump straight to the Queue.

Playlists
There are three kinds: the ones you build by hand, smart ones Luminous keeps filled from a rule you set, and Auto Playlists it builds and refreshes on its own.
•
Custom: create a playlist, name it, then drag songs in from anywhere in your collection.
•
Smart: set a rule like "Genre is Rock and Year is after 2015" and Luminous keeps it up to date as your collection grows.
•
Auto: Genre, Decade, and BPM playlists build themselves from your library and refresh daily with up to 25 tracks — each only shows up once it has at least 25 matching songs. Click one to browse its tracks, or hit Refresh anytime for a new selection.
BPM playlists sort songs into five fixed tempo categories using each song's BPM tag:

A custom playlist
Editing a smart rule
If you click the + next to a song or album, it goes to your Queue playlist by default. Making a new playlist you want to add to instead? Mark it Active first, and anything you + after that lands there instead.
You can also drag audio files or a whole folder straight onto the Luminous window to play or queue them — drop normally to replace the Queue and start playing, or hold Shift while dropping to append to the end of the Queue instead.

Managing a playlist
•
Double-click a playlist's name (or use its pencil icon) to rename it inline.
•
Drag rows to reorder a playlist — select several first to move them as a group. Click a column header to sort by it, or use the search field above the tracklist to filter down to matching songs.
•
Remove Duplicates and Remove Unavailable Tracks tidy a playlist in one click; Clear Playlist empties it; Delete Playlist removes it for good.
•
Queue and playlist tables can show a Date Added column via the column picker, alongside the others already there — handy for sorting a playlist by how recently each track landed in it.
Import & export
•
Bring in playlists from other apps, or send yours elsewhere, as .m3u, .m3u8, .pls, or .xspf.
•
Exporting offers a Relative Paths option, so the playlist still works after you move it to another folder or computer.

Lyrics
Open Lyrics in the sidebar to follow along with whatever's playing — the current line highlights in sync with the music, with the rest dimmed above and below it. Luminous automatically looks up lyrics for the currently playing song; if it's an instrumental, you can mark it as such so Luminous stops looking.
•
If lyrics haven't been found yet, click Refetch to look them up again.
•
Timing off, or a line wrong? Edit lets you correct the text or nudge the sync yourself.
•
Not all lyrics come with timestamps — when they don't, Luminous shows plain text lyrics instead.
•
Click any line in the lyrics to jump to that point in the song.
Advanced
Advanced Search
Typing field:value terms into the search bar turns a normal search into a precise filter across your whole library — no separate advanced-search screen to open. For example, genre:rock rating:>=4 finds rock songs you've rated 4 stars or higher.
Syntax
•
No operator on a text field (artist, album, genre…) means contains: genre:rock matches any genre containing "rock".
•
No operator on a numeric field (year, rating, bpm…) means exact match: year:1994 matches only 1994.
•
Multiple terms are combined with AND: rating:>=4 year:<2000 genre:rock — songs rated 4+ stars, released before 2000, in a genre containing "rock".
•
Bare words with no colon are free-text and OR'd against title/artist/album — mix them freely: rating:>=4 "tom cochrane".
•
Wrap a value with spaces in quotes: artist:"tom cochrane". Field names are case-insensitive.

Settings
General
App-wide preferences — things like startup behavior and other everyday defaults live here.
•
Language: switch the interface between English and French.
•
Rating style: choose whether songs are rated with a heart, or a 1–5 star scale — this is the setting the Now Playing section mentions.

Settings
Folders
Add or remove the folders Luminous watches for music. Once a folder's added, it stays watched — new files show up on their own, no rescan needed.
•
Watch folders in real time toggles whether Luminous watches live for changes, or only checks when you ask it to.
•
Rescan on startup has Luminous check your folders every time it launches.
•
Incremental Rescan looks only for what's changed; Force Full Scan re-reads everything from scratch. Both show live progress as they run.
•
A stats panel shows when your library was last scanned, plus running counts of songs, albums, artists, and total size.
Settings
Organize
Bulk tools for tidying up your library — cleaning up tags and files across many tracks at once, rather than one at a time. Prune Missing Songs clears out entries for files that no longer exist on disk, in one click.
The file-renaming tool works against your current Selection or the whole Library. Click Preview before anything moves: a From → To table lists every file with a status — OK, Unchanged, Collision, Missing Tag, or Error — with a filter to show only files that are actually changing, resizable columns, and a summary line totaling what will happen before you commit with Apply. A destination override sends the results to a different folder than the one you scanned, if you'd rather not reorganize in place. Extra toggles let you replace spaces with underscores, keep filenames ASCII-only, and move along extra files like cover art so they land in the new location too.

AcoustID / Chromaprint setup (optional)
To enable AcoustID audio fingerprinting and automatic metadata lookup, you need both the fpcalc utility and a valid AcoustID API key.
1. Install fpcalc
Linux (Ubuntu/Debian):
sudo apt install libchromaprint-tools
Windows: download the binary from the AcoustID website, extract it, and add the folder containing fpcalc.exe to your system PATH. Alternatively, set the FPCALC_PATH environment variable pointing directly to the binary.
2. Get and set an AcoustID API key
1
Register or log in to the AcoustID website.
2
Go to the My Applications page and register Luminous as a new application to generate a free Client API Key.
3
Paste the key into the AcoustID Integration field under Settings → Organize. Advanced users can instead set it as the ACOUSTID_API_KEY environment variable before starting the application — Luminous detects it automatically and skips the field.
Settings
Equalizer
Everything here lives under Settings → Equalizer. You don't need to know anything about audio engineering to use it — the defaults sound good, and every change applies instantly, even mid-song. The panel is split into three parts: the EQ itself, loudness normalization, and playback fades — each can be tuned or left alone independently.
•
Flip Enable EQ on, then pick a preset from the dropdown — Pop, Rock, Bass Boost, Vocal, and more — or dial in your own curve by dragging the band sliders. Switch to 20-band parametric if the default 10 bands aren't precise enough; it gives every band its own frequency and Q (bandwidth) instead of the fixed 10-band ladder, so you can carve out a narrow problem frequency without touching anything around it. A Reset Bands button appears in that view to snap everything back to flat in one click. The Preamp slider at the top raises or lowers overall gain to compensate before it hits the bands, so boosting doesn't clip.
•
Loudness Normalization evens out the volume jump between quiet and loud tracks, so you're not reaching for the volume knob every other song. Set a Target Level in LUFS, and choose whether to Normalize by track (each song hits the target on its own) or album (the whole album is scaled together, preserving the mix's intended dynamics between tracks). Luminous analyzes your library in the background — a progress message shows how many tracks are left — and falls back to embedded ReplayGain tags, then a flat Fallback Gain, for anything it hasn't measured yet.
•
Fades & crossfade softens the pause/stop button with a short Fade Duration instead of an abrupt cut, and can automatically blend the tail of one song into the start of the next over a Crossfade Duration you set. Preserve Gapless for Same Album keeps crossfade from kicking in between tracks on the same album — useful for live sets, concept albums, or anything mixed to flow without a gap.
10-band graphic — one slider per fixed frequency, fastest way to shape a curve by ear.
20-band parametric — free-placed bands with adjustable frequency and Q for surgical corrections.

UI Themes
Pick between several built-in themes, match your system's light/dark setting, or turn on Dynamic Artwork so the whole app — including Now Playing and the mini player — pulls its color from whatever album art is currently playing. The whole app updates immediately, no restart needed.
Dynamic Artwork doubles as a great way to discover new themes — when a song's artwork lands on a combination you like, save it as a Custom Theme to keep it around.
Want full control? The Custom Theme Builder lets you set your own colors from scratch.

Tips & tricks
Context menus & multi-select
Most lists in Luminous support right-click menus and multi-select, so you can act on many tracks at once instead of one at a time.
•
Right-click a song, album, or playlist row for quick actions — Play, Add to Playlist, Go to Artist/Album, Organize Files, Edit Tags, Remove.
•
Shift-click to select a range, Ctrl-click to add individual rows, and CtrlA to Select All in the current list view.
•
With a selection active, a floating toolbar appears with Play Selected, Add to Playlist, and Clear Selection.
